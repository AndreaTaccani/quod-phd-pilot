Research behind Quod

These studies informed how Quod helps doctoral researchers choose a next action, protect their attention, and close the day.

What the evidence covers

The studies examined specific practices and settings. Quod’s daily time budget, 21-day review, and exact timer settings are design choices. The pilot gathers feedback on using Quod. Its effects on productivity and wellbeing have yet to be evaluated.

Planning a goal

In Quod Quod’s WOOP-style form asks for a finish criterion, a likely obstacle, and a concrete five-minute response. Today keeps the action visible while the rest of the plan stays in Horizon.

  • Kappes & Oettingen (2011)Experiments

    Four experiments linked idealized positive fantasies to lower energization in the conditions studied. The experiments examined how people imagined future outcomes.

    Positive fantasies about idealized futures sap energy Journal of Experimental Social Psychology

  • Gollwitzer, Sheeran, Michalski & Seifert (2009)Experiments

    People acted less intensively on some identity-related intentions after someone else took notice of them. The experiments addressed intentions tied to how people saw themselves.

    When intentions go public: Does social reality widen the intention-behavior gap? Psychological Science

  • Gollwitzer & Brandstätter (1997)Studies and experiments

    Specifying when and where to act supported completion of difficult goals in the situations studied. One experiment assigned participants to make a concrete plan for a difficult task.

    Implementation intentions and effective goal pursuit Journal of Personality and Social Psychology

  • Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006)Meta-analysis

    Across 94 independent tests, forming if–then plans improved goal attainment on average. The studies covered a range of goals and settings.

    Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes Advances in Experimental Social Psychology

  • Wang, Wang & Gai (2021)Meta-analysis

    Across 21 studies, pairing a desired outcome with an obstacle and an if–then plan had a small-to-moderate average effect on goal attainment (g = 0.34, 95% CI 0.23–0.44). The authors flagged possible publication bias.

    A meta-analysis of the effects of mental contrasting with implementation intentions on goal attainment Frontiers in Psychology

Getting unstuck

In Quod The friction check-in offers a smaller action. Decide lets you reset a missed work date while keeping its deadline, and Quod draws estimate suggestions from your own comparable completed tasks. The quarterly and thesis views hold broader research questions.

  • Sirois & Pychyl (2013)Review and theory

    The review frames some procrastination as short-term relief from an unpleasant task, with costs deferred to a future self.

    Procrastination and the priority of short-term mood regulation: Consequences for future self Social and Personality Psychology Compass

  • Wohl, Pychyl & Bennett (2010)Longitudinal student study

    Among 119 first-year university students, more self-forgiveness for procrastinating before one exam was associated with less procrastination before the next.

    I forgive myself, now I can study: How self-forgiveness for procrastinating can reduce future procrastination Personality and Individual Differences

  • Buehler, Griffin & Ross (1994)Studies and experiments

    People underestimated their completion times on academic and everyday tasks. Explicitly relating estimates to relevant past experience reduced that optimistic bias in one experiment.

    Exploring the “planning fallacy”: Why people underestimate their task completion times Journal of Personality and Social Psychology

  • Trope & Liberman (2010)Review and theory

    The theory links greater psychological distance with more abstract representations. It informs the use of concrete actions for today and broader questions for later.

    Construal-level theory of psychological distance Psychological Review

Switching tasks and stopping

In Quod The parking lot records a specific next action and return time. The transition buffer gives you somewhere to write down unfinished work, and the off-hours shield leaves a capture field available after shutdown.

  • Leroy (2009)Experiments

    Two studies examined how thoughts about an earlier task can linger after a switch and interfere with work on the next task.

    Why is it so hard to do my work? The challenge of attention residue when switching between work tasks Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes

  • Leroy & Glomb (2018)Studies and experiments

    Making a brief plan for resuming interrupted work reduced attention residue in the conditions studied, including when participants expected time pressure on return.

    Tasks interrupted: Attention residue and the “ready-to-resume” plan Organization Science

  • Masicampo & Baumeister (2011)Experiments

    Making specific plans for unfinished goals reduced goal-related thoughts and interference in unrelated laboratory tasks.

    Consider it done! Plan making can eliminate the cognitive effects of unfulfilled goals Journal of Personality and Social Psychology

  • Smit (2016)Employee study and experiment

    Planning how to finish an incomplete goal improved detachment mainly among employees who typically had difficulty switching off after work.

    Successfully leaving work at work: The self-regulatory underpinnings of psychological detachment Journal of Occupational and Organizational Psychology

  • Sonnentag & Fritz (2015)Review and theory

    The model synthesizes workplace research on job demands, mentally disengaging in off-hours, and recovery.

    Recovery from job stress: The stressor-detachment model as an integrative framework Journal of Organizational Behavior

Focus and breaks

In Quod Timers and breaks are adjustable. The incubation mode offers a short, low-demand break and a place to record any insight when you return.

  • Biwer et al. (2023)Student study

    Among 87 students, break schedules differed on some experiences such as concentration and fatigue. Overall task completion showed no statistically significant difference.

    Understanding effort regulation: Comparing ‘Pomodoro’ breaks and self-regulated breaks British Journal of Educational Psychology

  • Smits, Wenzel & de Bruin (2025)Student study

    A two-hour study session involving 94 university students found no statistically significant differences in productivity, task completion, or flow across the three break approaches.

    Investigating the effectiveness of self-regulated, Pomodoro, and Flowtime break-taking techniques among students Behavioral Sciences

  • Albulescu et al. (2022)Systematic review and meta-analysis

    Across 22 independent samples, micro-breaks had small average benefits for vigor and fatigue. The overall performance effect was statistically uncertain.

    “Give me a break!” A systematic review and meta-analysis on the efficacy of micro-breaks for increasing well-being and performance PLOS ONE

  • Baird et al. (2012)Experiment

    A low-demand activity during a break improved subsequent performance on previously encountered creative-use problems in the experiment.

    Inspired by distraction: Mind wandering facilitates creative incubation Psychological Science

  • Sio & Ormerod (2009)Meta-analysis

    Setting a problem aside helped on average. The benefits varied with the problem and the conditions during the break.

    Does incubation enhance problem solving? A meta-analytic review Psychological Bulletin

Reading and recall

In Quod Quod asks you to recall a paper’s central claim, a limitation, and its relevance before saving a synthesis. It can then bring the note back for review.

  • Roediger & Karpicke (2006)Experiments

    Recalling prose passages after studying them improved retention on delayed tests compared with spending that practice time rereading.

    Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention Psychological Science

  • Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted & Pashler (2008)Experiment

    The study varied the interval between learning sessions and the final test. The most useful spacing depended on how long participants needed to retain the material.

    Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention Psychological Science

Doctoral wellbeing and motivation

In Quod Quod makes dependencies and milestone expectations explicit, offers private reflection, and shows finished work without ranking people. The companion server offers quiet co-working as a design inspired by the value of social connection in doctoral writing.

  • Levecque et al. (2017)Cross-sectional doctoral survey

    Among 3,659 PhD students in Flanders, reported distress was associated with work demands, control, work–family conflict, and supervision. These were associations measured at one time.

    Work organization and mental health problems in PhD students Research Policy

  • Steele, Boutouli, Swanson, Reid & Lane (2026)Doctoral interviews

    Twenty biology doctoral students at two US universities described how high-stakes evaluation, unwritten expectations, and comparison contributed to impostor feelings.

    Embedded gatekeeping, comparativeness, and hidden curriculum create feelings of impostorism for biology doctoral students CBE—Life Sciences Education

  • Vincent, Tremblay-Wragg & Plante (2023)Randomized doctoral study

    In a study of 100 doctoral researchers, a three-day writing retreat improved short-term self-reported wellbeing and distress compared with a waitlist. The study evaluated the whole retreat.

    Effects of a participation in a structured writing retreat on doctoral mental health: An experimental and comprehensive study International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health

  • Solms, van den Heuvel, Nevicka & Homan (2025)Randomized doctoral study

    After a multiweek workshop and practice period, doctoral participants in the self-compassion group reported more support-seeking than the waitlist group. Evidence for a change in work pressure was less certain.

    Be a hero, be your own best friend: A self-compassion-based PsyCap intervention improves PhD students’ well-being Higher Education

  • Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999)Meta-analysis

    Across 128 studies, some expected rewards contingent on completing a task reduced free-choice engagement on average. Positive feedback increased free-choice engagement and self-reported interest on average.

    A meta-analytic review of experiments examining the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation Psychological Bulletin

  • Ryan & Deci (2000)Review and theory

    The theory describes autonomy, competence, and relatedness as conditions supportive of intrinsic motivation and wellbeing.

    Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being American Psychologist
